Homelab

A datacentre that lives at home

One server, three GPUs, a dozen services — everything I use daily for AI, storage, media and code runs on hardware I can reach out and touch. No cloud subscriptions, no data leaving the house.

The hardware

The whole estate runs on a single machine: an AMD EPYC 7742 — 64 cores and 128 threads of Rome-era server silicon — with 94 GB of RAM and three Radeon RX 6700 XT cards providing roughly 30 GB of pooled VRAM. Consumer gaming GPUs doing datacentre work, coaxed along by ROCm. It is not the setup a cloud architect would draw on a whiteboard, and that is rather the point.

The software

Everything is a Docker Compose stack — one per concern, all joined by a shared bridge network, fronted by Caddy for internal TLS and published to the internet only through a Cloudflare Tunnel. No open ports on the router. Remote access for me is over Tailscale. Current project: production-grade vLLM serving with Prometheus/Grafana observability.

🧠

AI

Open-weight LLMs served by Ollama across the three GPUs, with Open WebUI for chat, agent pipelines, private metasearch, and speech in both directions — local text-to-speech and Whisper transcription.

Ollama · Open WebUI · SearXNG · Kokoro · Whisper
🗄️

Storage & backups

S3-compatible object storage on MinIO for everything that matters, with encrypted, versioned offsite backups so a house fire costs hardware, not history.

MinIO · restic · offsite replication
🛠️

DevOps

Self-hosted git with CI, container management, and a ROCm-enabled JupyterLab for when an idea needs a notebook and a GPU in a hurry.

Gitea · Portainer · JupyterLab
🎬

Media & monitoring

Jellyfin streams the media library with GPU transcoding, while Netdata watches every core, container and disk in real time.

Jellyfin · Netdata

Why bother?

Three reasons, in honest order. Privacy: my conversations with AI models, my photos, my documents — none of it leaves the building. Learning: operating real infrastructure teaches things no tutorial can; every outage is a seminar. Joy: there is a particular satisfaction in asking a question and hearing the GPU fans answer first.

I write about the lab occasionally on the blog — starting with why I self-host my own AI.
